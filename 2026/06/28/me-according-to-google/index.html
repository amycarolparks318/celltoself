Me, According to Google

The Woman Behind the Headline

If I was always judged by my worst day, I’d never be given a chance.

This was the moment every person with a sordid past and untold secrets dreads.

The moment you realize your worst day has arrived before you have.

Mine showed up on a Saturday morning in late January.

I had barely sat down at work when our HR Director walked into the office.

“Is there anything you want to tell me about your background?”

I froze. My stomach dropped. Dread and fear consumed me, and I had to remind myself to breathe.

Not because I had lied. Not because I had hidden my past. I hadn’t.

When I filled out my application, I checked the box.

Yes. I have a felony.

No one asked me about it. Not during the interview. Not after I was hired. Not once.

Until that Saturday morning.

The one thing I had feared the most since the day I walked out of prison was finally happening.

I had finally built a life I was beginning to love. A future I was excited about.

And in one conversation, I was convinced I was about to lose it all.

My mind was racing.

Did someone Google me? Did they find the article?

Does she know the final verdict or just the media’s version of my story?

Has she already judged me?

Has the decision about my future already been made for me again?

Suddenly, I wasn’t sitting in that office anymore. I was right back in prison. Scared to leave because I was terrified I’d fail.

The crazy part is that twenty-four hours earlier, I had told my boss everything.

Not because he asked. Not because I had to. Just because I was tired of carrying it around. So thankfully, he already knew.

Prison. Probation. Recovery. The addiction. The mistakes. All of it.

I held nothing back.

I thought I was finally taking control of my story.

I had no idea someone else was about to try to tell it for me.

Turns out the woman from the yellow house still had me in her sights and had decided to introduce me to my new employer.

Not by my work ethic.

Not by my professionalism.

Not by the woman I was becoming.

By my mugshot.

My news article had been emailed throughout the company.

The HR Director.

The company president.

My boss.

Members of my boss’s family.

Probably more people than I will ever know.

Anyone and everyone this person thought should know who I “really” was.

So there I was…

Where so many people leaving prison or rebuilding their lives eventually find themselves.

Holding my breath.

Panicking.

Waiting to find out whether my past had just stolen my future.

Thankfully…

It didn’t.

But that isn’t really why I tell this story.

I’m telling it because that morning taught me something I hope every person rebuilding their life hears loud and clear.

People can meet you in one of two ways.

They can meet your worst mistake.

Or they can meet the person you’ve become.

I couldn’t stop someone from finding my mugshot or the news article.

But I had already done something far more important.

I had told my story first.

And I’m not even talking about the conversation I had with my boss the day before.

I’m talking about my work ethic.

My professionalism.

My drive.

The way I showed up every single day.

I had gone above and beyond to prove my value long before anyone found my past.

So when the article finally showed up…

It wasn’t introducing me.

It was introducing someone I used to be.

That doesn’t mean it didn’t hurt.

It did.

That doesn’t mean shame didn’t come rushing back.

It did.

That doesn’t mean I wasn’t terrified.

I absolutely was.

For a few minutes, I had myself convinced one Google search was about to erase every blessing that had happened since prison.

But it didn’t.

Because God and recovery had already done what the headline never could.

They changed the person behind it.

The article hadn’t become untrue.

It had simply become incomplete.

My mugshot will probably outlive me.

The internet has a long-ass memory.

That woman in the article will always be part of my story.

She just doesn’t get to tell it anymore.

When my mugshot walked into that office that Saturday morning…

For the first time in my life…

It was no longer the loudest voice in the room.

The woman I had become sang louder.
