The Future I Was Afraid to Invest In

With Giving Tuesday approaching and my one-year anniversary of coming home from prison, Emma from Persevere reached out and asked if I'd share a little about my experience with their program and what life has looked like since my release and possibly feature my story.

Support Persevere for Giving Tuesday

Of course, I said yes! Persevere was a huge part of my journey, and I'm grateful for the opportunity to share what that experience meant to me and where life has taken me since.

1. Looking back, what led you to Persevere, and what initially motivated you to enroll?

Save money for my release or invest in a future I wasn't even sure I'd have? That was the choice I faced when I had to decide between work release and Persevere.

For someone preparing to leave prison without any financial support, that was a pretty tough decision.

I've always been passionate about technology and had experience with websites and coding, but I also knew that having those skills didn't necessarily mean anyone would give me a chance when I came home, especially if they Googled me.

Ultimately, I took a chance and invested in something that could help me build a future, not just get through the first few months. I chose Persevere, and looking back now, I'm so grateful I did.

2. What was your experience like going through the program?

Somewhere between my first line of code and my hundredth error message, I discovered that building websites and rebuilding confidence have a lot more in common than I ever imagined. I enrolled in Persevere to develop my own skills, but I never expected helping other people discover theirs would become one of my favorite parts of the program. After finishing the curriculum early, I had the opportunity to tutor other students and even teach Tech Tips classes.

When I got out, the Persevere staff surrounded me with support and encouragement. It was truly amazing and helped me get through the first few months, which were NOT all rainbows and butterflies.

3. Since graduating, what are some of the biggest milestones or accomplishments you've reached?

If the woman walking into Persevere could see my life today, I don't think she'd believe half of it. I walked out of prison hoping someone would give me a chance. A year later, I'm finding opportunities to create chances for other women. You can read more right here at Cell to Self!

After leaving prison sick of being surrounded by women, I am now helping manage a $13 million construction project and thoroughly sick of being surrounded by all men! Turns out, even in an industry I never imagined working in, the skills I learned at Persevere have come in pretty handy. I used vibe coding to build software my team thinks is genius.

Most recently, my sister and I have joined forces to create In the Clouds Technology! We're combining our experience and creativity to help small businesses build their online presence, and I'm getting to put even more of those coding and problem-solving skills from Persevere to work. And somewhere along the way, I adopted Willa, a scruffy little rescue dog who has absolutely no idea how much joy she's brought into my life.

4. As you reflect on the past year, what has changed for you personally or professionally?

For years, I measured my life by everything I'd lost. Today, I measure it by what I'm able to give. The biggest change in my life over the past year isn't what I've accomplished. It's who I'm becoming.

I'm learning to react differently to situations, keep things simple, and stay calm even when life doesn't go the way I planned. My relationship with God has become more intimate than ever, and I find myself constantly yearning to know Him better and become more like Him.

Recovery remains one of my top priorities. One of the greatest joys in my life right now is walking my sponsee through the AA steps. I also think it's important never to forget where I came from. I still keep in touch with about two dozen women I was incarcerated with, and I try to spread a little hope and encouragement wherever I can.

Rebuilding relationships with my children has been a slow process, but my two oldest are back in my life, and miracles continue to happen every day.

5. What would you say to someone who is considering enrolling in Persevere but may be unsure if they can take that next step?

Don't leave before the miracle happens! Even if that miracle is just figuring out why your code hasn't worked for the last six hours. Prison taught me patience. Coding made me question whether I'd actually learned any.

Take the leap. Push yourself. I promise you will grow in so many ways and create a support system beyond your wildest dreams. And who knows? The future you're so afraid to believe in might turn out to be better than anything you could have planned. Just look at me!
